Design report · codename khovax

Catch, don't fight.

A top-down, real-time ARPG on a hex grid. You catch monsters by walking rings around them. Your companions do the fighting, the simulation is fully deterministic, and co-op runs seeded, instanced maps on web, mobile and PC.

01Pillars

The player never attacks

You move, draw trails, place wards and trigger companion specials. Catching is the skill, and taking damage is the risk.

Deterministic everything

Integer math, fixed ticks, one seeded RNG. Same seed plus same inputs gives the same game, on every platform.

Gentle on the eyes

An extremely slow, fixed-angle camera. No screen shake, no zoom pulses. Motion comfort is a design rule.

One codebase, every platform

Godot 4 and GDScript only, shipping to web, mobile and PC from the same project.

02Core loop

  1. Enter a seeded instance
  2. Companions weaken a wild monster (false swipe)
  3. Walk a trail to close a ring
  4. Hold the seal: reinforce, place totems, feed berries
  5. Caught, or the ring breaks and the monster enrages
  6. Harvest threats for materials if needed
  7. Craft and grow your team

03Catching: the ring Decided

As the player walks, they leave a trail on the hexes. Closing a loop around one or more monsters starts a sealing phase. The ring has to hold for a number of ticks while every monster inside pushes against it. If the ring holds, everything inside is caught. If a wall segment fails, the ring breaks.

Bigger rings are weaker

Ring strength is spread across the perimeter, so a big ring has thin walls. A big ring is safe to draw from a distance, but monsters break out easily. A small ring is strong, but you have to stand close to the monster to draw it.

Pressure goes to the nearest wall

Every tick, each monster pushes on the wall segment nearest to it. Pressure scales with the monster's remaining HP, so weakening a monster first makes it much easier to hold. Many weak monsters or one strong one will overwhelm a big ring.

Rules as integer math Proposed numbers

ring_power     = 100 + level * 20 (+ bonuses from companion passives)
seg_integrity  = mul_div(ring_power, ONE, perimeter)       # thousandths of a power point
pressure(m)    = m.strength * ratio(m.hp, m.max_hp)        # thousandths too; false swipe lowers it
each tick:       every monster adds pressure(m) to its nearest wall segment
                 segment.damage >= seg_integrity  ->  ring breaks, everyone inside enrages
seal_ticks     = 40 + div_floor(total_pressure_inside, 10 * ONE)   # more resistance seals slower
co-op:           ring_power sums every player standing on the ring

Units Decided (#33): amounts (HP, strength, ring power, damage) are whole integers of at most 109, so any two multiply safely in 64 bits. Fractions are in thousandths (ONE = 1000 is 100 %), so wall integrity and pressure compare in the same unit. Every division rounds down, through Fixed (mul_div, ratio, div_floor): multiply first, divide last.

The structure is decided, but every constant is a placeholder to tune with the headless prototype. Player level scales ring power only moderately on purpose. The bigger power jumps should come from companion passives, so builds matter more than grinding.

Tactics this creates

  • Strong monster, small ring, close range. Reward and risk rise together without extra rules, and the health bar is what makes it tense.
  • Groups are a gamble. One big ring around five weak monsters is efficient but may burst. Several small rings are safe but slow.
  • Positioning before closing. Lure monsters toward the middle of the ring so their pressure lands on thick, far-away walls.
  • Active reinforcement Decided. While the seal runs, standing on a wall segment reinforces it, which is strong but puts you right where the monster is pushing.
  • Co-op herding. Trails from several players can join into one ring. One player herds, another closes it.

04Enrage Decided

Fleeing is boring, so a failed ring always enrages the monsters inside.

  • Enraged monsters get more strength and speed, partially heal, and target the player directly rather than companions. Decided
  • Enrage stacks with each further failed ring. Decided
  • Catching an enraged monster anyway grants a permanent trait such as "Tempered" with stronger passives, so failure becomes a high-risk opportunity. Decided

05Companions Decided

Combat behavior

  • False swipe by default. Companions fight on their own, but their basic attacks never take a wild monster below 1 HP.
  • Lethal toggle per companion. For threats you don't want to catch, or an enrage that gets out of hand.
  • Manual special attacks. The player triggers each companion's special attack. Specials can kill, which makes them a deliberate tradeoff. Decided
  • Killed wild monsters are gone for good. They turn grey (fainted), can't be caught, and drop crafting materials.

Species data model

Every species is pure data (Godot Resources or JSON), so new monsters don't need code changes and tests can validate every species automatically.

When a companion faints

A fainted companion is out for the rest of the instance and returns at the hub. An optional hardcore league with permadeath sits alongside.

06Player toolkit

The player's abilities come from companion passives and crafting, never from attacking directly. All of them plug into the ring system:

  • Enhanced warding: higher base ring power.
  • Totems: placed objects that raise the integrity of nearby wall segments.
  • Berries: lower a monster's strength or calm an enrage, but you have to get close to use them.
  • Spikes: the ring damages monsters pushing against it (false swipe rules apply).
  • Crafting: materials from harvested monsters become totems, berries and ring upgrades.

08World & co-op Decided

  • Instanced maps. A hub plus seeded instanced maps. Seed plus party inputs fully determine a run, so replays and shareable seeds come for free.
  • Single player is the default, with co-op for up to 4 players. In single player the simulation runs locally with no network layer.
  • Lockstep netcode. Clients send only inputs, with a small input delay. The slow pacing hides it well.
  • Transport: browsers only allow WebSocket or WebRTC. Start with a small WebSocket relay that only forwards inputs. Later it can run the same simulation headless to verify. Decided
  • Desync detection: clients compare state hashes every N ticks.

09Camera & comfort Decided

The camera is extremely slow for players prone to motion sickness. That makes comfort a hard design rule rather than a setting:

  • Fixed top-down angle, no rotation.
  • Low camera acceleration and no snapping. A dead zone means the camera only moves when you near the edge. Decided
  • No screen shake, no zoom pulses, no camera-driven hit effects. Impact is shown on the characters instead.

10Tech stack Decided

Godot 4 with GDScript only

As of September 2026, Godot C# projects still can't export to the web. The .NET WASM runtime has to be the main module, so Godot can't load it. A prototype existed in May 2025, but neither 4.7.2 nor the 4.8 dev snapshots ship it. Because the project wants one language everywhere and web is required, all code is GDScript, with static typing enforced through warnings as errors.

Architecture

  • Four strictly separated layers Decided (#26):
    • Input (input/): keys, the touch stick and later controllers press the same actions, which become one integer command per player per tick.
    • Simulation (sim/): the rules step the state from the commands, 20 ticks per second, in integers only. There are no nodes, time, input or floats, and CI enforces this.
    • Presentation (view/): scenes only read the state, and they interpolate between ticks so movement glides.
    • Driver (game/): runs whole ticks from real time. Real time decides when ticks happen, never what they do.
  • Every game rule is a modular feature Decided (#30): movement, the trail, and later catching, rings and monsters. An instance lists the features it has. Each one can be switched on or off, also during a run, so a boss stage can enable and disable mechanics on the fly. The core holds no game rule.
  • The starting state plus every tick's commands is a replay. The same replay always ends with the same state hash, which is the basis for lockstep co-op and desync detection.
  • Godot physics is never used for gameplay because it isn't deterministic across platforms.
  • All randomness comes from the run's seed, through integer generators (xoshiro128**) that live in the simulation state, one stream per feature (#32).
  • GDScript's 64-bit int is enough for fixed-point math: fractions in thousandths, every division rounded down (#33).

Rendering

  • Compatibility renderer everywhere. The web only supports Compatibility (WebGL2), so one look across all platforms means building for it from the start.
  • Comic-style cel shading: a toon ramp with 2–3 hard light bands, inverted-hull outlines and painted hatching textures. No screen-space edge detection, which is expensive or unavailable in Compatibility.
  • A slow, fixed camera keeps even weak phones comfortable.

Tooling

  • Installed through scoop on Windows: godot, gh, git, plus Godot export templates for web, Android and Windows.
  • GitHub for the repo, with GitHub Actions running headless Godot on Linux.
  • Claude-centric development. Species and content are data so they can be added without code changes.

11Testing Decided

Determinism makes end-to-end testing almost free, and heavy e2e testing is a core requirement.

  • An e2e test is a replay file (seed plus inputs per tick) with expected state hashes at checkpoints. Run headless, thousands of ticks take milliseconds.
  • Golden replays: "player catches a burrower in co-op" becomes a file. When a mechanic changes, the hash diff shows it immediately.
  • Desync tests: the same replay runs twice and on several platforms in CI, and the hashes must match.
  • Data validation: every species definition is checked for valid passives, specials and stats.
  • Framework: a minimal runner of our own to start with. Once there are five or more full flows of real game mechanics, switch to a framework such as GUT or gdUnit4. Decided
  • Web export smoke tests in CI.
